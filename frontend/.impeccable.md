## Design Context

### Users
Shippers (expedidores) — a genuine mix: occasional small-business owners (interior designer, aesthetic centre owner) who ship infrequently and need reassurance; and volume shippers (agricultural companies, logistics employees) who need speed and reliability. When filling out the offer wizard, they have already chosen a carrier and a transport window — they're past discovery, now in commitment mode. The job: fill in cargo details, get a cost estimate, send the offer, and trust that it lands correctly.

### Brand Personality
**Plain-spoken · Paper-grounded · Unhurried**
The system reads like an honest freight-office desk — no exclamations, no animations that demand attention. Trust is built through clarity (transparent pricing, capacity hints, visible window constraints) and restraint (one accent, one dark surface, never shouting).

### Aesthetic Direction
**"The Plain-Spoken Dispatch"** — defined in full in `frontend/DESIGN.md`.
- Light-first, warm-cream surfaces; Open-Sky Blue as the shipper voice.
- Unbounded 700 (display/labels) + Alegreya Sans 400 (body).
- One Deep Harbor accent, used sparingly (step numerals, eyebrows).
- Pill radius for interactive elements; 16–24px for containers.
- Anti-references: no neon-on-black, no gradient text, no left-stripe accents, no glassmorphism beyond the topbar's earned backdrop-filter.

### Design Principles (wizard-specific)
1. **Progress is earned, not announced.** The step indicator shows where you are without demanding attention — muted for upcoming, solid for current, checked for done.
2. **Constraints are a gift.** Weight/volume capacity hints and date bounds appear proactively so the user never hits a wall at submission.
3. **The cost estimate is the emotional anchor.** Step 3's estimated ARS figure should look authoritative — large, clearly formatted — so shippers feel informed, not surprised.
4. **Pending ≠ done, but it is meaningful.** The confirmation screen needs to communicate that the offer was submitted and is awaiting carrier acceptance — not finished, but real.
5. **Never beg for attention.** Transitions are fade + slight upward translate. No spring, no bounce, no scale. Reduced-motion users get none of it.
